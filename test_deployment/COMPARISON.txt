AXIS CONVENTION -- why their two numbers and our three are not comparable
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
A radiologist measures with a caliper on ONE axial slice, so the report quotes
the two IN-PLANE axes. 328 reports in the cohort state the order outright, e.g.
"6.8 x 5.5 mm (APxTR)". Across the sheet: 2646 report lines quote two
dimensions, 331 quote three.

We measure the 3D voxel extent, so we always have a third, cranio-caudal axis
the radiologist never took -- and for a ureteric stone that axis is often the
LARGEST, because the stone is elongated along a tube running head-to-foot.

Finding 3a, compared axis for axis instead of max to max:

    axis                       report    ours     diff
    AP  antero-posterior         3.7      4.4     +0.7
    TR  transverse               4.2      5.5     +1.3
    CC  cranio-caudal        not taken    7.2       --

So the apparent "+1.1 mm" in the scoring table was comparing their in-plane
maximum to our through-plane maximum. In-plane we are within about a millimetre.

This does NOT rescue finding 2. There the report says 9.1 x 15.4 mm, and 15.4 mm
exceeds EVERY axis we measured (TR 11.7, AP 7.6, CC 5.1, true 3D caliper 12.2).
That is a genuine ~3 mm underestimate on the obstructing stone, not an artefact
of axis convention.

The JSON now emits sizes in the report's own order and names the axes:
    "size_mm": "7.6 x 11.7 x 5.1 (AP x TR x CC)"
